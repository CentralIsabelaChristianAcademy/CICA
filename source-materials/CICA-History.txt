Great institutions often begin not with grand buildings or large crowds, but with a burden in someone’s heart.

In 1986, in the growing barangay of Marana 1st in the City of Ilagan, many young people faced a difficult reality. After finishing elementary school, their dreams quietly faded. High school education was far from home, transportation was costly, and daily travel meant riding overcrowded jeepneys along risky roads. For many families, continuing education felt like an impossible goal.
But God placed a vision in the hearts of two men — Mr. Eugenio V. Anza and Mr. Benjamin R. Dugay, Sr. After a board meeting of the Isabela Electric Cooperative (ISELCO II), what started as a simple conversation about education turned into something greater. They spoke not only about buildings and classrooms, but about the future of the youth. They saw the need. They felt the urgency. And they believed something had to be done.
Moved by compassion and faith, Mr. Anza made a bold and generous offer — he donated four hectares of prime land for the establishment of a secondary school in the area. It was not just a donation of property; it was a declaration of hope for the next generation.

The proposal was brought before the Board of Trustees of the Philippine Mission Churches of Christ of Northern Luzon. Under the leadership of President Mr. Charles W. Selby, the Board prayed earnestly and discussed the opportunity with great anticipation. They recognized that this was more than an educational project — it was a mission field. It was a chance to nurture minds and transform hearts for Christ.

With faith as their foundation, preparations began immediately. Pastor Rogelio F. Tenorio was called to help lay the groundwork. Permits were secured from the Department of Education, Culture and Sports (DECS). Teachers were recruited. Plans were drawn. Soon, two modest school buildings with three classrooms each rose from what was once open land.

Then, in June 1986, Central Isabela Christian Academy officially opened its doors. There were only 87 pioneering students during that maiden year. The facilities were simple. Resources were limited. But the vision was strong, and the faith was stronger. Those first students were not just enrollees — they were trailblazers. They carried the beginning of a legacy.

By its third year, CICA offered a complete secondary curriculum and earned government recognition. The first batch of graduates marched proudly, proving that the dream was not only possible — it was thriving. With each passing year, enrollment increased. Additional buildings and facilities were constructed. More families entrusted their children to the school. What once seemed like a distant dream became a living testimony in the heart of the community.

Today, Central Isabela Christian Academy stands as a symbol of what happens when vision meets generosity, and when faith is put into action. From 87 students to generations of graduates, from two small buildings to a growing campus, CICA continues to fulfill its mission of providing quality Christian education.

Its story reminds us that great beginnings often look small. That change begins with concern. That opportunity grows when people are willing to give. And above all, that when a vision is anchored in faith, God is faithful to bring it to completion.

The story of CICA is not just history.
It is a testimony.

And the story continues — written each year by every student, teacher, parent, and leader who walks through its gates.
For God’s honor and glory.
